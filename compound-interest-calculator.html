How the compound interest calculator works

This free compound interest calculator shows how an initial deposit grows when the interest it earns is added back to the balance and earns interest itself, and how regular monthly contributions add to that growth. Enter your starting amount, monthly contribution, annual interest rate, number of years, and how often interest compounds, and the future value, total contributions, and total interest update live, together with a year-by-year growth table. Everything runs in your browser, so your numbers are never uploaded to a server or saved anywhere.

How to use it

  1. Enter your initial deposit. This is the money you start with.
  2. Enter your monthly contribution, or leave it at 0 if you are not adding money each month.
  3. Enter the annual interest rate as a percentage, for example 7 for 7% per year.
  4. Choose the number of years, from 1 to 50, and the compounding frequency.
  5. Read the future value, total contributions, and total interest above the table, then scroll through the year-by-year breakdown to see how the balance grows each year.

What you can use it for

Retirement savings. Compare starting early with a small monthly contribution against starting later with a larger one, and see how many extra years of compounding change the final balance.

Savings goals. Work out how long it takes to reach a target amount, such as a house deposit or an emergency fund, at a given interest rate and monthly contribution.

Seeing the effect of the interest rate. Try the same deposit and contribution at 3%, 5%, and 7% to see how much difference a higher rate makes over 10, 20, or 30 years.

Frequently asked questions

How is compound interest calculated here?

The calculator simulates the balance month by month. Each month, your monthly contribution is added to the balance at the end of the month, and then one month of growth is applied at the effective monthly rate, which is (1 + r/n)^(n/12) - 1, where r is the annual rate as a decimal and n is the number of compounding periods per year. For example, $10,000 with $100 added each month at 7% per year compounded monthly for 10 years grows to $37,506.06: total contributions of $22,000 and interest of $15,506.06.

What is the difference between compounding annually and monthly?

Compounding more often means interest is added to the balance sooner, so it starts earning interest itself sooner. The difference is small over one year but grows over decades. With monthly compounding the effective monthly rate is derived from the annual rate and the compounding frequency you choose, so changing the frequency changes the result.

What happens if the interest rate is 0%?

With a 0% rate the balance simply adds up: the future value equals the initial deposit plus the monthly contribution times the number of months. For example, $5,000 with $50 a month for 5 years at 0% gives $5,000 + $50 x 60 = $8,000, with $0 of interest.

Does the calculator include taxes, fees, or inflation?

No. The results are before tax, before any account fees, and in nominal dollars, so they do not adjust for inflation. Real savings accounts and investments may pay less after tax and fees, and the same amount of money will buy less in the future if prices rise.

Is this financial advice?

No. The results are estimates for planning and education only, based on a fixed interest rate that never changes, and are not financial advice. Actual returns vary, and past or assumed rates do not guarantee future results. Consider speaking to a qualified financial adviser before making investment decisions.
