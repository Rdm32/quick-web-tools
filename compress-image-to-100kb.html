How the 100KB image compressor works

This tool works the other way round from an ordinary compressor: instead of asking you to guess a quality setting, you type the file size you need — 100 KB by default — and the tool finds the highest quality that still fits under that limit. A limit of 100 KB means 102,400 bytes or less, because here 1 KB is 1,024 bytes, which is how upload forms measure it. The search tries high qualities first and works downwards, so the downloaded file is always the best-looking version that meets your target. If even the lowest quality is still too big, and the dimensions option is on, the tool shrinks the pixel size step by step and searches again, because a sharp smaller picture looks far better than a blurry full-size one. Everything runs in your browser: images are decoded, re-encoded, and downloaded on your own device, and nothing is uploaded to a server. The maximum file size for each image is 20 MB, and re-encoding removes EXIF data such as the camera model and GPS coordinates.

How to use it

  1. Click Select Images and choose one or more JPG, PNG, or WebP files, each up to 20 MB. You can also drag files onto the upload area.
  2. Type your target size in kilobytes, or tap a preset such as 50 KB, 100 KB, 200 KB, 500 KB, or 1 MB. The default is 100 KB.
  3. Choose the output format: JPG for photos and forms, WebP for smaller files that keep transparency, or PNG for lossless graphics.
  4. Click Compress Images, check the before and after sizes for each file, then download the ones you need.

Frequently asked questions

How does the tool fit an image under 100 KB?

It searches for the highest JPG or WebP quality that produces a file at or under your target, starting near the top and working down. Only if the lowest quality is still too large does it reduce the pixel dimensions and search again. PNG output is lossless, so the quality search does not apply there: the tool only reduces the pixel dimensions until the file fits, or tells you honestly if it cannot.

Will compressing reduce the image quality?

A little, when the output is JPG or WebP, because both are lossy formats that discard fine detail to save space. The tool keeps the quality as high as your size limit allows, so at 100 KB most photos still look clean on a screen. If a result looks soft, try a larger target size, or a smaller pixel size at a higher quality, which often looks sharper than a large picture squeezed hard.

What happens to PNG images and transparency?

PNG is lossless, so a photo saved as PNG stays large: shrinking it means reducing its pixel dimensions, not its quality. Transparent areas survive in WebP and PNG output, but JPG has no transparency, so see-through parts become white. If your form only accepts JPG, transparency is lost whatever tool you use.

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. The whole job happens in your browser on your own device: files are read, re-encoded, and downloaded locally, and nothing is sent to a server. That makes the tool safe for ID photos, documents, and personal pictures, and it works offline once the page has loaded.

What if an image cannot reach the target size?

The tool downloads the smallest version it could make and says so plainly on that file, instead of pretending it fits. Very small targets, such as 5 KB for a detailed photo, may be impossible without making the picture tiny. In that case, allow the dimension reduction, pick a larger target, or resize the picture first and then compress it again.
